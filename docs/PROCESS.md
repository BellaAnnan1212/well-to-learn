# How this site is being made

I am Bella Annan and this is my project. This page explains how it is being built, so that anyone who asks me about it, at school or in an interview, gets the same answer I would give in person. It is written while the work happens, so it only ever describes what has been done as of the status line below, and it grows as the project does.

**Status as of 2026-09-04.** Plan approved (2026-09-03). Research storm filed and checked (2026-09-04). Three design directions drafted; the pick has not been made yet. Fifteen interviews done and their trimmed transcripts captured (2026-09-04); fifteen profile drafts exist, none approved yet, with their checks pending. No page is built and nothing is published.

## The idea

I wanted a public site at the meeting point of mental health and human rights, and I narrowed it to what I care about most: youth mental health, schools, and the right to education for young people who are disadvantaged. I chose the shape of the site myself: a world map you can explore, a plain-language guide to your rights, and a set of anonymous accounts from young people I interviewed. I set a deadline of mid-October 2026 so that the site would be live before my first university application.

## The plan came first

Before anything was built, a full plan was written and I approved it on 2026-09-03. It names every part of the site, the sources the map will use, the licence for each piece, the timeline, the risks, and, for each stage, the test that decides whether the stage is finished. Nothing was built until the plan was approved. I am using AI assistance (Claude) throughout, for research, code and design; the decisions in this document are mine and each one is dated in the project's decisions file.

## Research before claims

The site will make factual claims about mental health and about the law, and both are easy to get wrong. So the research ran as a "storm": five separate lines of argument (a practitioner's view, the academic evidence, a sceptic's view, an economist's view and a historian's view) plus a data scout who checked every candidate data source, its licence and its latest year. A contradiction map then listed where the lines disagreed and ruled on each disagreement by the strength of the evidence.

After that, a critic read every claim the site wanted to make and treated each as refuted until the primary source had been opened and the number, the population and the year matched. Any claim that failed was rewritten or dropped. That is why the site will say "an estimated one in seven" rather than a bare number, and why its rules forbid ranking any country by its suicide rate.

## Design

Three design directions have been drafted and written up, each with its colours, type and one distinctive idea. Before I choose, each one has to pass a contrast check and a check for whether it looks like every other site on the topic. I pick one at the design review; as of the status line above, the pick is still to come. The look of the site is a taste call, and taste calls are mine.

## The interviews

The interviews are done: I conducted all fifteen before the site existed. I trimmed each transcript myself and gave it one header line, and each one was then captured into a template that records an age band, a region, a schooling status, how consent was given, the main points, one or two quotes and what the person wants changed. No names, no schools, no towns are written down anywhere, not even in my private notes. Each profile will show a region rather than a country unless the person agreed to the country being shown, and the drawn avatars are chosen by a seed taken from the pseudonym, never to look like the person.

Every profile must pass four checks before I approve it: nothing in it narrows the person down; it follows the safe-messaging rules for writing about young people and mental health (no methods, no single-cause stories, help resources on every page); it contains nothing about me or about anyone I know; and it stays faithful to what the person actually said. As of the status line above, all fifteen profiles exist as drafts and none is approved yet. Anyone I interviewed can withdraw before the content freeze, and I will honour a request after it too.

## Approvals

Every page, every profile and every rights chapter is approved by me, with a date, before it goes live. Where I choose to accept a check that has not fully passed, that choice is written down with its reason. A teacher may review the site before launch and will be named on it only with their written agreement.

## Launch

The site will soft-launch first, then get a fresh design review, an accessibility check, a mobile and keyboard check and a fact check against every source; after that the content freezes for a final review before it is called done. After launch I should be able to fix a word from a browser and see it live within a couple of minutes; that is one of the tests the project has to pass, and it has not been run yet.

## What the AI does and does not do

The data pipeline, the code, the page design and the first drafts of the explanatory text are being built with AI assistance. The interviews, the questions, the framing, the choice of direction, and every approval are mine, and every number and legal claim on the site is checked against its primary source before it is published. The About page carries the same disclosure in one line.
