Your rights

What your country promised you about school and about your mind, in plain language: the treaty, the article, the line it comes from, where it tends to fail, and what you can ask for.

1 of 9 published

One chapter is published. The other eight are drafted and checked, and a draft that has been checked is still a draft.

No chapter goes up until every claim in it has been read back against the treaty, the general comment or the statute it comes from, and the quotation has been checked against the official text. A reviewer opens each source and begins from the assumption that the claim is wrong. A chapter is linked below only once it has passed that and Bella has approved it. Every other chapter says where it stands instead.

What the checking has already caught, so you can see it is real: a quotation that dropped the treaty's own words "shall strive to ensure" and so promised more than the treaty does; a sentence saying countries without a full ban "usually" have a partial one, when 53 of the 62 have none at all; a heading that made any refused adjustment discrimination, where the treaty says a reasonable one; a route to a hearing named with its two-year deadline left off; and, in the chapter about complaining to the UN, a treaty line protecting the reader's name that had been left out altogether.

All nine have been through a reviewer who opens every source and starts from the assumption that the claim is wrong. Chapters 2 to 9 were checked together and came back 321 of 369 rows on the first pass. Chapter 1 had been cleared at 30 of 30, then went through a stricter second check on 1 October 2026 before it was built as a page: that check refuted six more lines, and it took three rounds to close them. Almost none of the rows that failed in any chapter was a misquotation. They were places where a chapter spoke a committee's reading in its own voice, which is the mistake this kind of writing actually makes.

Each chapter is built the same way: the promise in one sentence, the instrument and the article it comes from, a short quotation from the official text with a link to it, what it means for you at school, where it most often fails, and what you can ask for.

This is information, not legal advice. Where a chapter describes a complaint route, it says plainly what that route can and cannot do.
