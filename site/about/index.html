About

Three questions a reader is owed an answer to: who made this, why, and how. The third is the longest, because a site that asks you to trust its numbers should show you how they were got.

Who made this

Well to Learn was made by Bella Annan, a secondary school student. She chose the subject, ran every interview, and made every editorial call on this site: which numbers appear, which are left out, what each profile says, and what the site refuses to claim.

She is not a doctor, a lawyer or a statistician, and this site does not pretend otherwise. It is a student's attempt to put reliable public data, and the actual words of the human rights treaties, somewhere a young person can read them.

Why

I tutor with Learn Like Me, a student-led 501(c)(3) in Indianapolis that works with elementary students in under-resourced schools. Sitting with the same child week after week, you stop being able to separate the two questions: whether a school is any good, and whether the person sitting in it is doing alright.

I wanted to know whether that held anywhere but my own city. It does, and the evidence is public, but it is scattered across agencies and written for governments. So this site puts what the numbers and the treaties actually say in one place, in plain words, for the person they are about.

How it was made

Every number in the Atlas comes from an agency that published it: the World Health Organization, the UNESCO Institute for Statistics, the World Bank, the United Nations Treaty Collection, End Corporal Punishment, and GCPEA. Nothing here is a number somebody remembered. Each value on a country card carries its own year, the kind of number it is, and who published it, because those three things are what make a number quotable.

Four words say what kind of number you are looking at. Measured means somebody counted it. Modelled means a model estimated it from other numbers. Self-reported means a government answered a questionnaire. Legal means it is what the law or the treaty record says. A country with no number is drawn in grey hatching and reads "no data". It is never drawn as a zero, because a missing number and a zero are different facts.

The site does not rank countries and carries no combined score. It draws no colour at all for how common mental illness is, for one reason: almost 90 per cent of countries have never measured it in children. A map coloured by that estimate would look like knowledge the world does not have.

The interviews are anonymous by design. No names, no schools, no towns, not even in the private files. Each person is described by an age band, a UN sub-region, and their schooling status in their own words. Consent was given by the young person and, where they are a minor, by a guardian; it is recorded to the month and never the day, because a set of exact interview dates read together is an itinerary. Anyone can withdraw. Every profile was read back against the interview notes, and against safe-messaging guidance from WHO and IASP, before it could appear.

Each legal claim in Your rights quotes the treaty itself and links to the certified text. Every one was checked by a reviewer whose standing instruction was to treat the claim as wrong until the source said otherwise. The same rule governs the data: five countries are read back against the live sources on every pass, and a value that cannot be confirmed is greyed out rather than shown.

Where AI helped, and where it did not

This is said plainly because you should be able to judge the site knowing it. Bella used an AI assistant (Claude) as a tool throughout. It wrote the code that fetches the data and builds these pages, drafted explanatory text including most of this section, and ran the checking passes described above. It did not conduct the interviews, and it did not decide what this site says.

Anything written in the first person about what Bella thinks, or why she made this, is hers. Where a draft is still waiting on her, the page says so in a dashed box rather than quietly printing something she did not write. The profiles are built from her notes of what real people said: no quotation is invented, and a paraphrase is labelled as one.

Correct this

If something on this site is wrong, say so and it gets fixed or taken down. That includes a number, a legal claim, a link, and anything in a profile that the person it belongs to wants changed or removed.
