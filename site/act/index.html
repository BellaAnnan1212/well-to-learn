Three things you can do

None of these needs money, an account, or anybody's permission.

  • Learn

    Find out what your own country promised. Nearly every country is bound by the Convention on the Rights of the Child, and most by the ICESCR, which together promise free primary education and health that includes the mind. Knowing which promises your country signed is the beginning of being able to ask for them.

    The chapter that walks through this is in Your rights, and is not written yet.

  • Ask

    Ask your school one question: who can a student talk to in confidence, and how does a student reach them without asking permission first? Ask a teacher, a year head or a student council. A school that cannot answer that question has found something worth fixing.

    Asked more than once, by more than one person, this question is how a name ends up on a door.

  • Share

    Share the rights guide once it is up, with one person who needs it more than you do. Share a voice rather than a statistic: a number tells somebody what is true, and a young person's own words tell them they are not the only one.

    Written content on this site is free to share under CC BY-NC 4.0. The interview profiles are not: link to them instead of copying them.
