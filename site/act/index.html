Four things you can do

None of these needs money, an account, or anybody's permission. None of them is a promise that anything will change: they are where asking starts.

  • Look up

    Find out what your own country promised. Open the Atlas, choose your country, and read the part of its card called "What this country promised": which of four treaties it has joined, and whether a young person there can take a complaint to the UN. Knowing which promises your country made is the beginning of being able to ask for them.

    The treaty record is read from the UN Treaty Collection and carries the date the UN stamped on its own page. What the promises mean at school is in Your rights.

  • Ask

    Ask your school one question: who can a student talk to in confidence, and how does a student reach them without asking permission first? Ask a teacher, a year head or a student council. A school that cannot answer has found something worth fixing.

    Ask it in writing if you can, and keep the answer. Before you tell anyone anything personal, ask who else will see it.

  • Share

    Send one page of this site to one person who needs it more than you do: your country's card in the Atlas, or a chapter of the rights guide once it is linked. One page to one person, with a line saying why you thought of them.

    Written content on this site is free to share under CC BY-NC 4.0, with credit and not for money. The interview profiles are not: link to them, never copy them.

  • Correct

    Tell this site where it is wrong. A number that has moved, a law that has changed in your country, a link that is dead, a sentence that says more than its source does. Every correction is checked against the source and, if it holds, the page is fixed or the claim is taken down.

    How to send one: Correct this. How the checking works: Methodology.

What this page does not ask

It does not ask for money, a signature, an email address or a share count. It does not tell you that sharing a page fixes anything, or that asking a question will get you an answer. It does not ask you to tell your own story to anyone, here or anywhere else.

This is information, not medical or legal advice. If today is not a day for doing any of this, that is fine. The help lines are below.
