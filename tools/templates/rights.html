No chapter goes up until every claim in it has been read back against the treaty, the general comment or the statute it comes from, and the quotation has been checked against the official text. A reviewer opens each source and begins from the assumption that the claim is wrong. A chapter is linked below only once it has passed that and Bella has approved it. Every other chapter says where it stands instead.

What the checking has already caught, so you can see it is real: a quotation that dropped the treaty's own words "shall strive to ensure" and so promised more than the treaty does; a sentence saying countries without a full ban "usually" have a partial one, when 53 of the 62 have none at all; a heading that made any refused adjustment discrimination, where the treaty says a reasonable one; a route to a hearing named with its two-year deadline left off; and, in the chapter about complaining to the UN, a treaty line protecting the reader's name that had been left out altogether.

This is information, not legal advice. Where a chapter describes a complaint route, it says plainly what that route can and cannot do.
